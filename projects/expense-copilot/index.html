Corporate Card Expense Copilot

American Express is launching an AI expense-management platform for business card customers. This prototype does the core jobs on real company-card data: categorise every purchase, catch policy breaches such as split purchases and duplicate charges, and hand a manager a short, ranked review queue with the reason for each flag.

    Method

    Data: District of Columbia purchase-card transactions (DC Open Data, CC BY 4.0), Jun 2023 – Sep 2026, pulled from the city's ArcGIS REST service. Fields: agency, date, amount, vendor, vendor state, merchant category. There is no cardholder ID, so rules run at agency level, and there are no fraud labels. Limits from DC Public Schools purchase-card guidance ($5,000 goods, $2,500 services). → Categories: 348 merchant category descriptions mapped to 13 expense categories with keyword rules → vendor-name classifier (TF-IDF char 2–4-grams, Logistic Regression, GroupShuffleSplit by vendor) → 7 policy rules in pandas + 9 SQL queries (CTEs, CASE, UNION ALL, window functions: SUM OVER, RANK, moving average) → Isolation Forest on 7 features (amount vs category median, vendor and category rarity within the agency, out-of-state vendor, etc.) vs a z-score baseline, scored on weak labels → Benford first-digit test with Nigrini's MAD thresholds → risk score = rule points + 4 × anomaly percentile.
